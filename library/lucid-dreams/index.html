    Lucid Dreams¶

    A dream where one becomes aware they are dreaming.

    Techniques¶

    Research Studies¶

    • Lucid Dreaming Verified by Volitional Communication During REM Sleep (LaBerge, S., et al. 1981) - Provided the first physiological verification of lucid dreaming using pre-agreed eye movement signals during REM sleep.
    • Lucid Dreaming Treatment for Nightmares: A Pilot Study (Spoormaker, V. I., & van den Bout, J. 2006) - Randomized 23 nightmare sufferers to individual, group or waiting-list conditions; one 2-hour lucid dreaming treatment session reduced nightmare frequency at 12 weeks, but becoming lucid was not necessary for the improvement.
    • Induction of Lucid Dreams: A Systematic Review of Evidence (Stumbrys, T., et al. 2012) - Reviewed 35 studies of lucid dream induction techniques and found that none were verified to induce lucid dreams reliably and consistently, although some looked promising.
    • Neural Correlates of Dream Lucidity Obtained From Contrasting Lucid Versus Non-Lucid REM Sleep: A Combined EEG/fMRI Case Study (Dresler, M., et al. 2012) - In the one participant (of four) with lucid REM episodes long enough to analyze, lucid dreaming activated the precuneus, parietal lobules, and prefrontal and occipito-temporal cortices compared with non-lucid REM sleep.
    • Measuring Consciousness in Dreams: The Lucidity and Consciousness in Dreams Scale (Voss, U., et al. 2013) - Developed the LuCiD scale, with eight factors including insight, control, thought, memory and dissociation, now a standard outcome measure in lucid dreaming studies.
    • Induction of Self Awareness in Dreams Through Frontal Low Current Stimulation of Gamma Activity (Voss, U., et al. 2014) - Reported that 25 and 40 Hz transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) during REM sleep induced self-reflective awareness in dreams, while other frequencies did not.
    • Metacognitive Mechanisms Underlying Lucid Dreaming (Filevich, E., Dresler, M., et al. 2015) - Found greater frontopolar (BA9/10) gray matter volume in people reporting high dream lucidity, and stronger activation of the same region when they monitored their own thoughts while awake.
    • Lucid Dreaming Incidence: A Quality Effects Meta-Analysis of 50 Years of Research (Saunders, D. T., et al. 2016) - Estimated that 55% of people have had at least one lucid dream and 23% have them monthly or more.
    • Effectiveness of Motor Practice in Lucid Dreams: A Comparison With Physical and Mental Practice (Stumbrys, T., Erlacher, D., & Schredl, M. 2016) - In 68 participants, practicing finger tapping in a lucid dream improved next-morning performance about as much as physical or waking mental practice.
    • Reality Testing and the Mnemonic Induction of Lucid Dreams: Findings From the National Australian Lucid Dream Induction Study (Adventure-Heart, D. J., et al. 2017) - Compared reality testing, WBTB and MILD in 169 participants and found that combining all three induced lucid dreams, especially when participants fell asleep soon after practicing MILD.
    • Lucid Dreaming: Intensity, But Not Frequency, Is Inversely Related to Psychopathology (Aviram, L., & Soffer-Dudek, N. 2018) - In 187 students, lucid dream frequency was unrelated to psychopathology, while deliberate induction predicted increases in dissociation and schizotypy over two months.
    • Pre-Sleep Treatment With Galantamine Stimulates Lucid Dreaming: A Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Crossover Study (LaBerge, S., LaMarca, K., & Baird, B. 2018) - In 121 participants combining WBTB and MILD, 4 mg and 8 mg of galantamine, a prescription drug, raised the share reporting a lucid dream to 27% and 42%, compared with 14% on placebo.
    • Frequent Lucid Dreaming Associated With Increased Functional Connectivity Between Frontopolar Cortex and Temporoparietal Association Areas (Baird, B., et al. 2018) - Found stronger resting-state connectivity between the anterior prefrontal cortex and temporoparietal regions in frequent lucid dreamers, but no differences in brain structure.
    • The Cognitive Neuroscience of Lucid Dreaming (Baird, B., Mota-Rolim, S. A., & Dresler, M. 2019) - Reviewed EEG, neuroimaging, lesion, drug and brain stimulation studies, finding EEG results mostly underpowered and mixed, prefrontal and parietal regions implicated, and cholinergic stimulation combined with mental set training the most promising induction approach.
    • Findings From the International Lucid Dream Induction Study (Adventure-Heart, D. J. 2020) - Compared five combinations of reality testing, WBTB, MILD and SSILD in 355 participants and found MILD and SSILD similarly effective, regardless of prior lucid dreaming experience.
    • Wake Up, Work on Dreams, Back to Bed and Lucid Dream: A Sleep Laboratory Study (Erlacher, D., & Stumbrys, T. 2020) - Waking participants after six hours to practice MILD for 30 to 60 minutes produced lucid dreams in 36 to 54% of unselected participants, compared with 9% after reading.
    • Attempted Induction of Signalled Lucid Dreaming by Transcranial Alternating Current Stimulation (Blanchette-Carrière, C., et al. 2020) - Repeated tACS lucid dream induction with stricter methods and found signal-verified lucid dreams after both real and sham stimulation.
    • Real-Time Dialogue Between Experimenters and Dreamers During REM Sleep (Konkoly, K. R., et al. 2021) - Demonstrated that real-time communication with lucid dreamers is possible via eye movements and facial muscle signals.
    • Two-Way Communication in Lucid REM Sleep Dreaming (Baird, B., LaBerge, S., & Tononi, G. 2021) - Commentary on Konkoly et al. 2021 that discusses whether longer two-way conversations with lucid dreamers are feasible.
    • Lucid Dreaming Occurs in Activated Rapid Eye Movement Sleep, Not a Mixture of Sleep and Wakefulness (Baird, B., Tononi, G., & LaBerge, S. 2022) - Found that signal-verified lucid dreams occur during REM sleep with higher eye movement density, and that the reported 40 Hz "hybrid state" signal was an eye movement artifact.
    • Benefits and Concerns of Seeking and Experiencing Lucid Dreams: Benefits Are Tied to Successful Induction and Dream Control (Mallett, R., et al. 2022) - Analyzed posts from a large lucid dreaming forum and found that lucid dreams can end nightmares, while negative outcomes mostly followed failed induction attempts or low dream control.
    • Combining Presleep Cognitive Training and REM-Sleep Stimulation in a Laboratory Morning Nap for Lucid Dream Induction (Carr, M., et al. 2023) - Introduced targeted lucidity reactivation (TLR): participants learned to associate sound and light cues with a lucid mindset before a morning nap, and replaying the cues in REM sleep produced signal-verified lucid dreams in 50% of participants, compared with 17% without cueing.
    • A Systematic Review of New Empirical Data on Lucid Dream Induction Techniques (Tan, S., & Fan, J. 2023) - Updated Stumbrys et al. 2012 with 19 newer studies covering 14 techniques and found MILD the most effective, with SSILD and galantamine promising but needing replication.
    • The Effectiveness of Lucid Dreaming Therapy in Patients With Nightmares: A Systematic Review (Ouchene, R., et al. 2023) - Found 4 randomized controlled trials and several case studies; most reported fewer nightmares in adults, but internal validity was limited.
    • Targeted Lucidity Reactivation Implemented in an Open-Source watchOS App (Mallela, R., & Mallett, R. 2024) - Developed an open-source iOS/watchOS application that runs targeted lucidity reactivation at home, using Apple Watch motion and heart rate data to time cues to REM sleep.
    • Provoking Lucid Dreams at Home With Sensory Cues Paired With Pre-Sleep Cognitive Training (Konkoly, K. R., et al. 2024) - Moved targeted lucidity reactivation (TLR) to a smartphone without polysomnography. Lucid dreaming increased compared with the prior week and with blinded control nights, but only when the sleep cues matched the pre-sleep training sounds.
    • The Clinical Neuroscience of Lucid Dreaming (Tzioridou, S., et al. 2025) - Reviewed links between lucid dreaming and nightmare disorder, depression, anxiety, psychosis and dissociation, and the potential drawbacks of lucid dreaming interventions.
    • Electrophysiological Correlates of Lucid Dreaming: Sensor and Source Level Signatures (Demirel, Ç., et al. 2025) - Pooled EEG across labs and found minimal sensor-level differences between lucid and non-lucid REM sleep, but reduced right parietal beta power and more alpha and gamma connectivity during lucid dreaming.
    • Inducing Lucid Dreaming With Multisensory Stimulation: A Preregistered Multi-Center Study (Picard-Deland, C., Jafarzadeh Esfahani, M., et al. 2026) - Preprint. Combined pre-sleep SSILD training with light, sound and vibration cues during REM sleep in 60 participants across three labs. Cueing did not make lucid dreams more frequent than sham, but roughly doubled the duration of signal-verified lucid dreams.

    Research Labs¶

    • Donders Sleep & Memory Lab - Martin Dresler's lab at the Donders Institute in Nijmegen, studying the neuroscience of sleep, dreaming and lucid dreaming.
    • Paller Lab - Ken Paller's cognitive neuroscience lab at Northwestern University, behind the two-way dream communication and at-home TLR studies.
    • Dream Engineering Lab - Michelle Carr's lab at the Université de Montréal, developing sensory stimulation methods to induce lucid dreams.
    • Dream and Nightmare Laboratory - Tore Nielsen's lab in Montreal, studying dreams, nightmares and sleep.
    • Institut für Bewusstseins- und Traumforschung - Brigitte Holzinger's institute in Vienna, offering research, seminars and lucid dreaming therapy for nightmares.

    Datasets¶

    • DREAM Database - Dream EEG and Mentation database pooling 20 datasets with 505 participants and 2,643 awakenings that pair sleep EEG with dream reports.
    • DreamBank - Searchable collection of more than 20,000 dream reports from research studies and dream journals.
    • Sleep and Dream Database - Kelly Bulkeley's archive of dream reports and survey data for research.

    Books¶

    • Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming by Stephen LaBerge - Workbook of step-by-step exercises for learning to lucid dream, based on research at Stanford University.
    • Lucid Dreaming: A Concise Guide to Awakening in Your Dreams and in Your Life by Stephen LaBerge - Short guide that distills LaBerge's research at Stanford and the Lucidity Institute into practical techniques.
    • Lucid Dreaming: Gateway to the Inner Self by Robert Waggoner - Proposes five stages of lucid dreaming, with advice for beginners and for experienced lucid dreamers who want to go further.
    • Lucid Dreaming, Plain and Simple by Robert Waggoner and Caroline McCready - Short, practical introduction to lucid dreaming techniques and what to do once lucid.
    • Are You Dreaming?: Exploring Lucid Dreams: A Comprehensive Guide by Daniel Love - Comprehensive modern guide to the science, techniques and practice of lucid dreaming.
    • A Field Guide to Lucid Dreaming: Mastering the Art of Oneironautics by Dylan Tuccillo - Illustrated step-by-step guide to becoming lucid and what to do once lucid.
    • Learn to Lucid Dream: Powerful Techniques for Awakening Creativity and Consciousness by Kristen LaMarca - Methodical introduction to the science and practice of lucid dreaming, and to applying it in waking life.
    • Llewellyn's Complete Book of Lucid Dreaming by Clare R. Johnson - Comprehensive guide to lucid dreaming techniques and to using lucid dreams for creativity, nightmares and sleep problems.
    • Dreams of Awakening by Charlie Morley - Lucid dreaming and mindfulness of dream and sleep, combining Western techniques with Tibetan Buddhist practice.
    • Far Journeys by Robert A. Monroe - Robert Monroe's account of his out-of-body experiences, the sequel to Journeys Out of the Body.
    • The Tibetan Yogas of Dream and Sleep by Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche - Guide to Tibetan dream yoga and sleep yoga, whose goal is recognizing the nature of mind.
    • Dream Yoga by Andrew Holecek - Combines Western lucid dreaming techniques with the Tibetan yogas of dream and sleep.
    • Dream Yoga and the Practice of Natural Light by Chogyal Namkhai Norbu - Dzogchen teachings on developing clarity in sleep and dreams, including practices that go beyond lucid dreaming.

    Apps¶

    Android¶

    • Lucid Scribe - Dream journal, Homing Signal cue training, guided MILD, SSILD, WBTB and FILD, targeted lucidity reactivation, and REM detection with the INSPEC device.
    • Awoken - Dream journal, reality checking, dream cues, dream patterns and achievements.
    • Dream Catcher - Dream journal with lucidity and clarity ratings, dream pattern statistics, nightmare tracking and reality check reminders.
    • Oniri - Dream journal with voice recording, dream interpretation and lucid dreaming lessons.
    • Sleep as Android - Smart alarm clock with sleep cycle tracking.
    • Lucidity - Dream journal with AI dream interpretation, guided MILD, WILD, SSILD and WBTB lessons, and reality check reminders.
    • TritonSleep - Private on-device dream journal, reality-check reminders and guided MILD, WBTB, SSILD, WILD and FILD sessions with evidence ratings; no account.

    iOS¶

    • Lucid Scribe - Dream journal, Homing Signal cue training, guided MILD, SSILD, WBTB and FILD, targeted lucidity reactivation, and REM detection with the INSPEC device.
    • DreamWell - Dream journal, notifications, and lucid dreaming and mindfulness courses.
    • Lucid Dreaming, Mindfully - Guided course that teaches lucid dreaming through mindfulness practice.
    • Shape - 30-day guided program for learning to lucid dream.
    • Oniri - Dream journal with voice recording, dream interpretation and lucid dreaming lessons.
    • Lucidity - Privacy-minded dream journal with tags, moods, journaling prompts and beginner guides to lucid dreaming.
    • TritonSleep - Private on-device dream journal, reality-check reminders and guided MILD, WBTB, SSILD, WILD and FILD sessions with evidence ratings; no account.

    Lucid Dreaming Devices¶

    • INSPEC - A night-vision smart camera that detects eye movements during REM sleep and triggers audio and visual alerts to promote lucidity.
    • ZMax LD Set - Research-grade EEG headband that detects REM sleep and plays light, sound and vibration cues, used in lab studies of lucid dream induction.

    Open-Source Projects¶

    • Dreamento - Python toolbox for dream engineering that records, monitors and analyzes EEG in real time.
    • Dream Journal App - A Dream Journal that uses Google Drive.
    • lucidsourcekit - An all-you-need toolkit for lucid dreaming including a binaural beats player and much more.
    • Lightened Dream - A dream journal designed for lucid dreamers.
    • Dormio - Influences the semi-lucid state of mind known as hypnagogia, which occurs just before falling asleep, by playing audio cues.
    • SSILD Trainer - A SSILD (Senses Initiated Lucid Dream) training web app designed to help you practice the SSILD cycles.
    • TLR - Open-source iOS/watchOS app for targeted lucidity reactivation, with Apple Watch sleep staging to time cues to REM sleep.
    • YASA - Python package for sleep analysis, including automatic sleep staging of polysomnography recordings.
    • ezscore - Artifact-aware sleep stage classifiers for forehead EEG headbands such as the ZMax.

    Mindfulness Exercises¶

    • UC San Diego Center for Mindfulness - Guided exercises on mental noting, awareness of breath, and body scan.
    • UCLA Mindful - Guided exercises on breath, sound, and body awareness.
    • The Free Mindfulness Project - Guided exercises on breath, sound, body awareness and self guided exercises.
    • AudioDharma - Guided exercises on giving attention and appreciating awareness of the moment.

    Audio Programs¶

    • Lucid Dreaming Series - This four-exercise series is designed to teach you how to program and consciously participate in your own personal dreamscape.
    • Discover Your Worlds Of Dreams - Meditative sessions specifically designed to help you to have lucid dreams which are helpful and relevant to your life situations.

    Forums¶

    • r/LucidDreaming - Large community for sharing induction techniques, questions, dream reports, resources and research news.
    • DreamViews - All about lucid dreaming, controlling dreams, lucid aids, sleep stages and dream signs.
    • LD4all - One of the oldest lucid dreaming communities, with a forum, a free beginner's guide, and technique tutorials.
    • Attrape-Songes - French forum with interesting experiments worth translating.
    • Klartraumforum - German forum with interesting experiments worth translating.

    Discord Servers¶

    • OmniLucid - A knowledgeable space with active events that consistently provide new videos, guides, and stories pertaining to lucid dreaming.
    • Lucid-Dash - A welcoming community of lucid dreamers of all skill levels, active chat with experienced lucid dreamers, tons of resources, videos and tutorials to help you lucid dream.
    • Dream Café - A place to share your dream stories, get help and hang out with experienced lucid dreamers from around the world.
    • Tech for Dreaming - Chat about using technology to make lucid dreaming more accessible.
    • Klartraumland - The German server for lucid dreaming, with a scientific background, dream reports and discussions, livestreams, and gaming.

    YouTube Channels¶

    • Daniel Love - Easy to follow tutorials and expert advice from one of the world's leading lucid dreaming teachers.
    • TIGER123 - Dives into techniques for inducing lucid dreams, exploring dream symbolism, and enhancing dream control, with a mix of tutorials, personal experiences, and insights from the latest dream research.
    • Tipharot - All about lucid dreaming and similar subjects.
    • Charlie Morley - Talks and guided practices from a lucid dreaming teacher who combines Western techniques with Tibetan Buddhist dream yoga.

    Podcasts¶

    • The Lucid Dreaming Podcast by Lucid Sage - Covering theory, philosophy, practice and gadgets for lucid dreaming, with interviews with researchers including Benjamin Baird, Karen Konkoly and Denholm Aspy (archive, last episode 2022).
    • The Dream World - Weekly show on lucid dreaming, sleep and consciousness, with dream researchers, lucid dreamers and personal lucid dream experiments.
    • Sleep & Deep Lucid Dreaming - Dr. Clare Johnson on lucid dreaming techniques, nightmares and dream yoga.

    Meetups and Conferences¶

    • IASD Annual Conference - Yearly conference of the International Association for the Study of Dreams, with research talks and lucid dreaming workshops.
    • Tech for Dreaming - Occasional online talks and meetups on technology for lucid dreaming.

    Magazines¶

    • Lucid Dreaming Experience - Free, reader-supported quarterly magazine of lucid dream reports and articles by lucid dreamers.

    Games¶

    • DARQ - An adventure game that tells the story of Lloyd, a boy who becomes aware of the fact that he is dreaming.
    • Dare to Lucid Dream - A story game about Sonny, who meets someone inside his lucid dreams.
    • Yume Nikki - Free exploration game set in the strange, dark world inside a girl's dreams.
    • Superliminal - First-person puzzle game about escaping a surreal dream world by bending perspective.

    Movies¶

    • Waking Life - Animated film in which a young man drifts through a series of dreams, discussing consciousness and lucid dreaming with the people he meets.
    • Vanilla Sky - A man whose life unravels after a disfiguring accident slowly realizes that the world he inhabits is a lucid dream.
    • Paprika - Animated film about a device that lets therapists enter their patients' dreams, and what happens when it is stolen.
    • Inception - A thief who enters people's dreams is hired to plant an idea in a target's mind.
    • Lucid Dream - A journalist uses lucid dreaming to search his memories for clues to his son's kidnapping.
